"Monsoon" comes from an Arabic word meaning "season". It describes a wind system that changes direction between summer and winter, and it is the most important weather event for hundreds of millions of people in South Asia.

## The basic idea: land warms faster than sea

In summer the land heats up much faster than the ocean. Warm air over the land rises, leaving an area of low pressure. Cooler, moist air from the Indian Ocean rushes in to fill the gap. As it moves over land it rises, cools, and drops its water as rain.

In winter the pattern reverses. Cold, dry air flows from land to sea, bringing mostly dry weather to most of India.

## The southwest monsoon

The summer monsoon usually reaches Kerala around the start of June and spreads across the country by early July. It then retreats from the northwest from September. Together these months bring roughly three-quarters of India's yearly rainfall.

### Two branches

- **Arabian Sea branch:** hits the Western Ghats first, giving heavy rain on the west coast.
- **Bay of Bengal branch:** moves toward the northeast and then west along the Himalayan foothills.

## Mountains matter

Mountains force moist air upward, which cools it and produces heavy rain on the windward side. This is why the Western Ghats are very wet on their western slopes and much drier to the east. The Khasi Hills in Meghalaya catch the Bay of Bengal winds, and Mawsynram there is often cited as the wettest place on Earth.

## Why it matters

1. **Farming:** crops such as rice, pulses and cotton depend on monsoon timing.
2. **Water:** rivers, reservoirs and groundwater are refilled.
3. **The economy:** a weak monsoon can push up food prices.

A good monsoon is not only about the total amount of rain. Gaps of dry weeks in the middle of the season, or very heavy rain in a few days, can still harm crops and cause floods.
