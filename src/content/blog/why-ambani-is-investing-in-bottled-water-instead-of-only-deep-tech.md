# Why Is Mukesh Ambani Investing in Bottled Water Instead of Only Deep Tech? The Business Strategy Behind Reliance's Move

**By Mukesh Kumar | 25 November 2026 | Business & Strategy**

Imagine having access to billions of dollars in capital, an established business empire, millions of customers and the resources to invest in almost any industry in the world.

What would you do?

Would you invest in artificial intelligence, robotics, semiconductor manufacturing, biotechnology or futuristic technologies that could transform the global economy?

Or would you enter a business as ordinary as bottled drinking water?

This is precisely the question that makes Reliance Industries' entry into India's packaged drinking water market interesting.

Through its consumer products business, Reliance has introduced Campa Sure, entering a market already occupied by established players such as Bisleri, Kinley and Aquafina.

At first glance, this might seem like an unusual investment decision for a conglomerate led by one of the world's wealthiest businessmen, Mukesh Ambani.

Why invest in selling something as basic as water when the company has the financial resources to pursue much more technologically advanced opportunities?

Is Reliance looking at something that ordinary consumers and entrepreneurs are overlooking?

The answer may have less to do with the bottle itself and much more to do with distribution, market access, economies of scale, customer relationships and long-term capital allocation.

Let's examine the business thinking behind this move.

## Quick Summary: What Is the Thinking Behind Ambani's Bottled Water Investment?

Reliance's entry into the bottled water market should not be viewed simply as an investment in another consumer product.

It fits into a broader strategy of expanding its consumer business, strengthening its distribution network and building a diversified portfolio of businesses.

Here are the key ideas:

* **Distribution advantage:** Reliance can potentially use its existing retail and distribution ecosystem to reach consumers at scale.
* **Market opportunity:** Packaged drinking water is an established market with recurring consumer demand.
* **Economies of scale:** Selling multiple products through overlapping distribution channels can improve operating efficiency.
* **Consumer ecosystem:** FMCG products can increase the company's presence in everyday consumer spending.
* **Long-term investments:** Reliance is also investing in areas such as artificial intelligence, renewable energy, advanced manufacturing and research.

**The central idea:** A company does not always need to invent a revolutionary product to build a large business. Sometimes, the competitive advantage comes from controlling how efficiently an existing product reaches millions of customers.

## 1. Why Is Reliance Entering the Bottled Water Business?

Reliance's packaged drinking water initiative is part of its larger consumer products strategy.

Its Campa Sure brand entered a category where consumers already have established purchasing habits.

People purchase bottled water while travelling, shopping, working, attending events and visiting restaurants. Unlike many new technology products, packaged water does not require the company to educate consumers about an entirely new need.

The demand already exists.

According to industry estimates reported by *Financial Express* in 2025, India's packaged drinking water market was valued at approximately ₹24,000 crore, with a significant portion still operating in the unorganised segment.

That creates an interesting opportunity for a company with extensive retail and distribution capabilities.

### The importance of pricing

Reliance introduced Campa Sure with competitive pricing. Its reported introductory pricing included ₹15 for a one-litre bottle.

This matters because bottled water is a highly competitive category.

When consumers perceive two products as sufficiently similar, price, availability, trust and convenience can influence their purchasing decisions.

However, lower prices alone do not guarantee success. A brand must also maintain consistent product quality, regulatory compliance and consumer confidence.

Reliance's opportunity is to combine competitive pricing with distribution reach.

## 2. The Real Business Strategy: Distribution Is More Powerful Than You Think

Consider two hypothetical entrepreneurs.

Entrepreneur A develops a revolutionary product.

They spend several years on research, develop a prototype, invest heavily in manufacturing and finally bring the product to market.

But after developing the product, they still need to find customers, establish distribution and convince retailers to sell it.

Entrepreneur B takes a different approach.

They identify a product that millions of people already purchase, establish manufacturing partnerships, develop a strong brand and build a distribution network.

Which entrepreneur has an easier path to reaching customers?

In some industries, Entrepreneur B may have a considerable advantage because the market already exists.

This is an important aspect of Reliance's consumer business strategy.

### Reliance's existing ecosystem

Reliance already operates across several consumer-facing businesses.

These include:

* Reliance Retail stores.
* JioMart's online commerce platform.
* Consumer brands such as Campa.
* Warehousing and logistics infrastructure.
* Relationships with manufacturers, distributors and retailers.

Now imagine introducing another product through an established consumer ecosystem.

Instead of building an entirely new distribution system, the company can potentially use existing relationships and infrastructure.

This creates opportunities for economies of scale.

A company distributing only bottled water must recover its distribution costs through water sales.

A company distributing bottled water, soft drinks, packaged food and household essentials can potentially use overlapping distribution infrastructure for multiple product categories.

This is an important distinction.

**The product might be ordinary, but the distribution system behind it can become a competitive advantage.**

## 3. The Strategy of Capturing Everyday Consumer Spending

There is an important business concept called *share of wallet*.

It refers to the proportion of a customer's total spending that a company captures.

Think about the products an average household purchases regularly:

* Drinking water.
* Milk.
* Packaged food.
* Snacks.
* Biscuits.
* Personal care products.
* Household essentials.

Each category represents a separate consumer spending opportunity.

Now imagine a company that wants to become part of a customer's purchasing decisions across multiple categories.

That is a much larger opportunity than selling a single product.

Reliance's consumer business strategy appears focused on building a diversified portfolio of everyday products.

The company has publicly described its ambition to build a large consumer brands organisation offering affordable products across major consumer categories.

This approach has two potential advantages.

First, multiple products can increase the number of purchasing occasions involving the same company.

Second, a broader product portfolio can improve the economics of distribution by spreading logistics and retail costs across categories.

### Why this matters

A company selling one product depends heavily on the success of that product.

A company selling dozens of products across multiple consumer categories can diversify its revenue sources and build stronger relationships with retailers and customers.

This is ecosystem thinking rather than isolated product thinking.

## 4. Why Not Invest Everything in Artificial Intelligence, Robotics or Deep Technology?

This is perhaps the most interesting part of the discussion.

Why would a company with enormous financial resources enter traditional consumer industries instead of concentrating on futuristic technology?

The answer lies in the economics of different businesses.

Deep technology and consumer businesses have different investment requirements, risk profiles and commercial timelines.

Consider a company investing in advanced semiconductor technology.

It may require specialised research, expensive equipment, highly skilled engineers, intellectual property and years of development before commercial returns become meaningful.

Now consider a packaged drinking water business.

The product category already exists, consumers understand it and the manufacturing process is established.

The challenge is to compete on quality, pricing, branding and distribution.

Neither business is automatically superior.

They simply serve different strategic purposes.

### A diversified investment portfolio

Large conglomerates often operate across different industries because they have different investment horizons.

Some businesses may generate established operating cash flows.

Others may require significant upfront capital but create future opportunities.

A diversified portfolio allows a company to participate in several markets without depending entirely on one industry's performance.

This does not mean every consumer business will be profitable or every technology investment will succeed.

It means capital allocation should be considered across the entire business portfolio rather than through a single investment decision.

## 5. Is Reliance Actually Investing in Deep Technology?

Yes. The idea that Reliance is choosing bottled water instead of deep technology would be an incomplete interpretation of its overall investment strategy.

Its annual reporting and public announcements document investments in several technology-intensive areas.

### Artificial intelligence and data centres

Reliance has announced major ambitions in AI infrastructure, including large-scale data centre development.

AI infrastructure requires computing capacity, specialised hardware, energy, cooling systems and reliable connectivity.

This is a different type of investment from traditional consumer products, but it can create capabilities for future digital businesses.

In November 2025, Reuters reported Reliance's plans for a one-gigawatt AI data centre in Andhra Pradesh, alongside its planned AI infrastructure development in Jamnagar.

### Renewable energy and new energy technologies

Reliance has also been developing capabilities in:

* Solar manufacturing.
* Battery storage.
* Green hydrogen.
* Electrolysers.
* Fuel cells.
* Advanced energy materials.

These industries involve manufacturing, engineering, research and long-term infrastructure development.

The company has announced an ambition to build substantial renewable energy capacity as part of its new energy strategy.

### Research and development

Reliance's FY 2024–25 annual report describes its ambition to develop advanced manufacturing and deep-tech capabilities.

It reported more than 1,000 scientists, engineers and specialists working across its innovation ecosystem.

As of 31 March 2025, the company reported 1,156 granted patents.

This indicates that its investments are not limited to conventional consumer products.

### Advanced manufacturing and consumer technology

Even its FMCG strategy involves more than simply buying products from manufacturers and selling them.

Reliance has described investments in in-house research and development, backward integration, manufacturing infrastructure and supply-chain improvements.

This is important because innovation does not always mean inventing an entirely new technology.

It can also mean improving manufacturing efficiency, product quality, logistics, packaging and affordability.

## 6. The Bigger Strategy: Control the Value Chain

To understand Reliance's approach, consider the value chain of a bottled water business.

It includes several stages:

**1. Sourcing:** Obtaining water from permitted and suitable sources.

**2. Processing:** Purification, treatment and quality control.

**3. Manufacturing:** Bottling, packaging and production.

**4. Branding:** Building consumer recognition and trust.

**5. Distribution:** Transporting products to retailers, supermarkets and other sales channels.

**6. Customer access:** Making the product available whenever consumers need it.

A company that develops competitive advantages across several stages can strengthen its position in the market.

However, it does not necessarily need to own every stage.

For example, Reliance can work with regional bottling partners while concentrating on branding, quality standards, pricing and distribution.

This can reduce the need to build every manufacturing facility independently.

The broader business concept is value-chain integration.

It is about understanding where the greatest competitive advantages exist and deciding which parts of the business to own, operate or partner with others.

### Why distribution can become a competitive moat

A competitive moat is an advantage that makes it difficult for competitors to replicate a company's market position.

Distribution can become such an advantage when it is supported by:

* Strong retailer relationships.
* Efficient logistics.
* Large purchasing volumes.
* Brand recognition.
* Reliable product availability.
* Cost-efficient operations.

A competitor might be able to manufacture a similar bottle of water.

Replicating an extensive distribution ecosystem, however, can be much more difficult.

That is why distribution deserves as much attention as product innovation when studying large consumer businesses.

## 7. What Can Indian Entrepreneurs Learn From This Strategy?

There are several useful lessons here, particularly for entrepreneurs who assume that the biggest business opportunities must involve futuristic technology.

### Lesson 1: Do not underestimate traditional industries

Water, agriculture, food processing, logistics, construction and healthcare may not always look technologically exciting.

But these industries serve enormous markets.

A business does not need to invent a new human need to create value.

It can solve an existing problem more efficiently, affordably or reliably.

### Lesson 2: Distribution can be as valuable as innovation

Entrepreneurs often spend most of their time developing products.

But a product that cannot reach customers has limited commercial value.

A strong distribution strategy can help an ordinary product compete against technologically superior alternatives.

### Lesson 3: Build businesses around existing infrastructure

If you already have a customer base, supplier relationships, warehouses or retail channels, you may be able to enter adjacent product categories more efficiently.

This is one reason established companies can have advantages over new entrants.

### Lesson 4: Think beyond individual products

Instead of asking, "How can I sell more bottled water?", consider asking:

"How can I build a distribution system that can sell ten different products?"

That question changes the scale of the opportunity.

### Lesson 5: Think in different investment horizons

Not every business needs to generate returns immediately.

Some investments can target established demand, while others can focus on technological capabilities that may take years to commercialise.

A thoughtful business strategy considers both.

## 8. Is Ambani Sending a Message to India's Business Community?

We should distinguish between documented statements and our interpretation of business decisions.

There is no reason to assume that the bottled water investment was intended as a specific message to Indian entrepreneurs.

However, Reliance's publicly stated consumer business ambitions do communicate certain strategic priorities.

One recurring theme is making global-quality products available at affordable prices.

Another is building manufacturing capabilities and distribution infrastructure within India.

These priorities have implications for Indian businesses.

They suggest that opportunities exist not only in developing new technologies but also in improving the efficiency of industries that already serve millions of people.

For entrepreneurs, this raises an important question:

Are we overlooking large opportunities because we are too focused on businesses that look innovative rather than businesses that solve real problems?

A packaged water company may not appear futuristic.

But the systems required to manufacture, maintain quality, transport and distribute millions of bottles efficiently involve significant operational complexity.

That is a legitimate business opportunity.

## 9. The Three Time Horizons Behind a Large Conglomerate's Strategy

One useful way to understand Reliance's portfolio is to divide its opportunities into three time horizons.

This is an analytical framework, not a confirmed internal investment formula.

### Short-term opportunities: Consumer businesses

Examples include:

* Packaged drinking water.
* Soft drinks.
* Packaged food.
* Everyday essentials.
* Retail.

The strategic objective is to build consumer reach, recurring demand and a diversified revenue base.

### Medium-term opportunities: Infrastructure and manufacturing

Examples include:

* Data centres.
* Logistics.
* Manufacturing facilities.
* Batteries.
* Energy infrastructure.

The objective is to develop capabilities and infrastructure that can support multiple businesses over time.

### Long-term opportunities: Deep technology

Examples include:

* Artificial intelligence.
* Advanced materials.
* Biotechnology.
* Renewable energy technologies.
* Advanced manufacturing.

The objective is to participate in industries that could transform the economy over the coming decades.

The interesting point is that these opportunities do not necessarily compete with one another.

They can potentially reinforce each other.

For example, AI infrastructure can improve supply-chain management, manufacturing operations and retail forecasting.

Similarly, an established consumer business can provide a platform for testing new products and technologies.

## 10. The Important Risks That Should Not Be Ignored

A balanced business analysis must also examine the challenges.

Reliance's entry into the packaged water market does not guarantee commercial success.

There are several factors worth watching.

**Competition:** Established brands already have consumer recognition and distribution relationships.

**Profit margins:** Competitive pricing can put pressure on profitability, particularly when transportation and packaging costs are significant.

**Quality and regulation:** Packaged drinking water is subject to food safety and quality requirements. Maintaining standards across a large network of manufacturing partners is essential.

**Environmental sustainability:** Bottled water businesses depend on responsible water sourcing and generate plastic packaging waste. Groundwater management, recycling and packaging choices matter.

**Consumer trust:** A low price may encourage trial purchases, but long-term customer loyalty depends on consistent quality and availability.

**Execution:** A large distribution network creates an advantage only when it operates efficiently.

These challenges show why market entry and long-term market leadership are two different things.

## Conclusion: The Real Business Is Not Always the Product

The most interesting part of Reliance's bottled water investment is not the bottle itself.

It is the larger business strategy that may sit behind it.

A company can participate in traditional consumer industries while simultaneously investing in artificial intelligence, renewable energy, advanced manufacturing and research.

These activities are not necessarily contradictory.

They represent different ways of allocating capital, developing capabilities and participating in the economy.

For entrepreneurs, the bigger lesson is to look beyond the product.

Study the market, understand the value chain, examine distribution economics and identify where a lasting competitive advantage can be created.

Sometimes, the biggest opportunity is not inventing something that the world has never seen.

It is taking something the world already needs and building a better system around it.

**Because in business, owning a great technology is one kind of advantage. Knowing how to deliver value to millions of customers is another. And the most powerful businesses may be those that learn to combine both.**

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Why is Mukesh Ambani investing in bottled water?

Reliance's entry into packaged drinking water fits its broader strategy of expanding its consumer products portfolio. The company can potentially leverage its retail presence, distribution relationships and manufacturing partnerships to compete in an established market.

### 2. What is Campa Sure?

Campa Sure is a packaged drinking water brand introduced by Reliance Consumer Products Limited. It is part of Reliance's broader FMCG expansion.

### 3. Is Reliance investing in artificial intelligence and deep technology?

Yes. Reliance has announced investments and initiatives involving AI infrastructure, data centres, renewable energy, advanced manufacturing and research and development.

### 4. Why is distribution important in the bottled water business?

Bottled water is a high-volume consumer product. Availability, logistics, pricing, retailer relationships and product quality can influence purchasing decisions. A strong distribution network can therefore become a significant competitive advantage.

### 5. Is bottled water a profitable business?

It can be profitable, but profitability depends on manufacturing efficiency, sourcing, packaging costs, transportation, pricing, distribution margins and competition. Large sales volumes do not automatically guarantee high profits.

### 6. What can startups learn from Reliance's FMCG strategy?

Startups can learn to study existing markets, identify distribution gaps, develop partnerships, improve operating efficiency and build competitive advantages beyond the product itself.

### 7. Is Reliance following a Jio-like strategy in the bottled water market?

There are similarities worth examining, particularly competitive pricing and the ambition to expand market reach. However, the bottled water market has different economics, competitors and operational requirements. It is too early to assume that the results will replicate Jio's telecom experience.
