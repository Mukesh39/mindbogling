A topographic map shows the shape of the land on a flat sheet of paper. Its secret is the **contour line**: a line joining all points at the same height above sea level.

Survey of India publishes topographic sheets for the country, and similar maps exist almost everywhere.

## Rule 1: closer lines mean steeper ground

When contour lines are packed tightly together, the ground rises quickly. When they are far apart, the slope is gentle.

## Rule 2: circles are hills (or pits)

Closed rings with rising numbers toward the centre mark a hill or peak. Small tick marks pointing inward on a ring mean a depression.

## Rule 3: V-shapes point uphill along streams

Where contour lines cross a stream, they bend into a V that points upstream, toward higher ground. That tells you where valleys run and which way water flows.

## Quick reference

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Lines very close together | Steep slope or cliff |
| Lines far apart | Gentle slope or flat land |
| Nested closed rings | Hill or mountain |
| Bold line with a number | Index contour, giving the height |
| V pointing uphill | Valley or stream |

## Find the contour interval

The map legend gives the **contour interval**, the height difference between neighbouring lines, for example 20 metres. Count the lines between two points and multiply by the interval to estimate how much you will climb.

> Before any hike, trace your route with your finger and count the lines you will cross.

## Practice

Open any topographic map of an area you know. Find a hill, a valley and the steepest slope. Then check your guesses against what you remember on the ground.
